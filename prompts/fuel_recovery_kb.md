# Fuel and recovery knowledge base

Purpose: a fixed set of entries that turn what a personal training or movement review found (late-session fade, soreness, heavy legs, bad sleep after games, pain notes) into an explanation, a concrete action sized for this player, a check that tells him whether it works, and the source behind it. It goes deeper than the skill KB's recovery entry, which points here.

Audience: one adult male player, about 30, 173 cm, 76 kg, office job, background in distance running. Rec league, about one evening game a week, plus the 8-week program (`program/2026-10-20/`) with four sessions a week: A braking strength / plyo, B court movement (speed, deceleration), C skill with the trainer, D power + conditioning. Eats mostly Chinese food at home. **Assumption:** he lives in Australia, so the AIS supplement framework, Sports Dietitians Australia (SDA), Sport Integrity Australia (SIA) and the Australian Food Composition Database (AFCD) are used; the research notes call this "likely", not confirmed.

How agents use it:
- The movement and training report writers cite entries by slug (`kb: ["FR-game-day"]`) and use an entry only when the session data, the player's notes or his log show the **Situation** (for example a "legs gone in the 4th" note, next-morning pain scores, a sleep log, a weigh-in). If the situation is not in the data, do not use the entry.
- Copy the numbers from **What to do** as written (already scaled to 76 kg). Re-scale only if his logged body mass changes by more than about 2 kg (our rule).
- **Check** is what goes into the plan or log so the next review can see whether it worked.
- **Never give medical advice beyond red-flag referral.** No diagnosis of the calf, knee or anything else; pain patterns are "possible", "likely" or "unclear". Red flags send him to a sports physiotherapist or doctor (FR-soreness-vs-pain). Before a big change to diet or supplements, the notes advise an Accredited Sports Dietitian (SDA directory).
- No weight cut during the 8-week block (FR-body-comp). Do not write a deficit into any plan in that period.

Limits:
- Energy numbers come from a prediction equation (individual error likely +/-10-15 %); food values are approximate (AFCD, labels vary). The real check is the weekly weight trend plus training quality.
- The causes of his calf soreness, knee aches and late-game fade are unknown. Under-fuelling and dehydration are plausible contributors to the fade, not established (unverified).
- Labels: **Evidence level** is Strong (position stand, consensus or several RCTs/meta-analyses agree), Moderate (some RCTs or a meta-analysis with caveats), Weak (small single studies, mechanism, expert opinion). "(our rule)" marks our own practical choice built from the evidence but not tested as such; present it to the player as our choice, not "the science says". "unverified" marks figures the research notes did not re-check.

Main sources (short names used below):
- **Joint Stand 2016**: ACSM / Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics / Dietitians of Canada, *Nutrition and athletic performance*, Thomas, Erdman & Burke 2016, J Acad Nutr Diet 116(3):501-528, DOI 10.1016/j.jand.2015.12.006.
- **IOC 2018**: Maughan et al., IOC consensus on dietary supplements, Br J Sports Med 52(7):439-455, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2018-099027.
- **AIS**: AIS Sports Supplement Framework, Group A, https://www.ausport.gov.au/ais/nutrition/supplements/group_a
- **ISSN**: nutrient timing (Kerksick 2017, DOI 10.1186/s12970-017-0189-4), creatine (Kreider 2017, DOI 10.1186/s12970-017-0173-z), caffeine (Guest 2021, DOI 10.1186/s12970-020-00383-4).
- **SDA**: *Food for your sport: basketball*, https://www.sportsdietitians.com.au/factsheets/food-for-your-sport/food-for-your-sport-basketball
- **ACSM 2007**: Sawka et al., fluid replacement position stand, Med Sci Sports Exerc 39(2):377-390, PubMed 17277604.

## Entries

### FR-energy-by-day

**Situation:** Late-session or late-game fade, "feels heavy", flat sessions, constant hunger or weekly weight drifting; or any plan that needs a daily intake target.

**Why:** Needs differ a lot between a desk day and a game day. Basketball is stop-start and uses muscle glycogen heavily; the Joint Stand 2016 says to "fuel for the work required" and to move carbohydrate so availability is high for key sessions. Chronically low energy availability impairs hormones, bone, immunity and training adaptation, in men too (RED-S).

**What to do (76 kg):**

| Day type | Energy (kcal, starting point) | Carbs g/kg | Carbs g/day | Protein | Fat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rest / desk day | 2,350-2,550 | 3-3.5 | 230-270 | ~120 g | 60-100 g |
| Strength or power day (A, D) | 2,700-2,950 | 4-5 | 300-380 | ~120 g | 60-100 g |
| Court / skill day (B, C; day before the game) | 2,750-3,000 | ~5 | ~380 | ~120 g | 60-100 g |
| Game day | 2,850-3,100 | 5-6 | 380-450 | ~120 g | 60-100 g |

Energy = Mifflin-St Jeor RMR (~1,700 kcal) x 1.4-1.5 for an office job, plus the session cost (hard basketball about 600-800 kcal/h for 76 kg, less with bench time; strength session about 250-400 kcal net, our estimate). The g/kg split inside the Joint Stand bands is our rule. Fat at 20-35 % of energy; cut oil and fried dishes first if intake must drop, never protein or session carbs (our rule).

**Check:** 3-4 morning weigh-ins a week, compare weekly averages: steady weight plus good session quality = about right (our rule). Watch for low-energy signs: falling performance, constant fatigue, poor sleep, low libido, frequent illness, injuries that will not settle.

**Source:** Joint Stand 2016 (carb bands 3-5 g/kg low/skill, 5-7 g/kg ~1 h/day moderate; fat 20-35 %; low energy availability); Burke et al. 2011, J Sports Sci 29 Suppl 1:S17-S27, DOI 10.1080/02640414.2011.585473; Mifflin et al. 1990, DOI 10.1093/ajcn/51.2.241; basketball cost https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3154898/table/pone-0021822-t001

**Evidence level:** Strong for the bands and the principle; Moderate for the energy estimates; the per-day split is our rule.

### FR-protein

**Situation:** Strength and plyo load is rising (sessions A, D), soreness lingers, or the food log shows protein mainly at dinner.

**Why:** Protein added to resistance training raises strength and fat-free mass, with no further fat-free mass gain above about 1.6 g/kg/day. Spreading it over the day keeps muscle protein synthesis topped up; total daily protein matters more than exact timing.

**What to do (76 kg):** ~1.6 g/kg = **~120 g/day as 4 feeds of ~30 g** (about 0.4 g/kg): breakfast, lunch, after training / dinner, pre-bed. Pre-sleep option: 300-500 mL milk or a yoghurt (casein-rich, 30-40 g protein in the ISSN wording) on training nights or when dinner was early. Plant protein works; with tofu or soy milk use a slightly larger serve or pair it with egg, dairy or meat. Examples: 3 eggs + 250 mL milk ~27 g; 150 g cooked chicken ~45 g; 200 g firm tofu ~25-30 g; 400 mL soy milk ~13-15 g.

Cue: "A palm and a half of protein, four times a day." / 中文："每天四次，每次一个半手掌大小的蛋白质。"

**Check:** Food log for 3 days: 4 feeds of 25-40 g each (our rule). Soreness after A/D settles within the normal DOMS window (FR-soreness-vs-pain).

**Source:** Morton et al. 2018, Br J Sports Med 52:376-384, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2017-097608 (49 RCTs, plateau ~1.62 g/kg); Joint Stand 2016 (1.2-2.0 g/kg; 0.3 g/kg after key sessions and every 3-5 h); Schoenfeld & Aragon 2018, DOI 10.1186/s12970-018-0215-1 (0.4 g/kg/meal over >= 4 meals); ISSN Kerksick 2017 (20-40 g every 3-4 h, pre-sleep protein, plant leucine).

**Evidence level:** Strong (daily target); Moderate (per-meal split, pre-sleep).

### FR-carbs-timing

**Situation:** Evening sessions after a desk day; he trains on a 1 pm lunch alone; fade in the last third of a session; gut trouble in sessions.

**Why:** Glycogen at the start of a stop-start session matters more than what he takes during it. Fat, fibre and protein close to the start slow digestion and can upset the gut.

**What to do (76 kg):**
- **Main meal 1-4 h before** sessions over 60 min: 1-4 g/kg carbs (~75-300 g; in practice 1.5-2 bowls rice, ~100 g carbs, 3-4 h before), lower fat and fibre, moderate protein.
- **Snack 1-2 h before:** carbohydrate-based, low-fat, ~40-80 g carbs (mantou, banana + soy milk, toast with honey).
- **0-30 min before, only if hungry:** half a banana, a few lollies, sips of sports drink (our rule).
- **Fluid** 5-10 mL/kg (~400-750 mL) in the 2-4 h before, pale-yellow urine.
- **After:** a normal carb-containing meal within a couple of hours plus ~25-30 g protein (0.3 g/kg) within ~2 h. "Speedy refuelling" (1.0-1.2 g/kg/h for 4 h = ~75-90 g/h) is only needed when the next demanding session is < 8 h away, which his week never has.
- Never train an evening session on lunch alone: add the 4-5 pm snack (our rule).

**Check:** Session RPE and the last-third numbers in the review (shooting `thirds`, rep rate) on fuelled vs unfuelled days; no gut complaints (our rule).

**Source:** Joint Stand 2016 (1-4 g/kg 1-4 h before; 5-10 mL/kg fluid; 0.3 g/kg protein after; speedy refuelling < 8 h); SDA basketball (low-fat carb snack 1-2 h before); ISSN Kerksick 2017 (wider "anabolic window").

**Evidence level:** Strong for the guideline numbers; the food choices and the "no lunch-only evening session" rule are our rule.

### FR-game-day

**Situation:** Any game day, especially a 7-9 pm game after work; notes like "legs gone late", "no energy in the 2nd half", or bad sleep after games.

**Why:** Starting the game with full glycogen and fluid matters most for a 40-min rec game. Carbs during play help most when he starts low on fuel; a carb-electrolyte drink did not beat water in well-hydrated players. Caffeine helps sprints and jumps but costs sleep at evening games.

**What to do (game at ~8 pm, 76 kg, 380-450 g carbs for the day):**

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| Breakfast | Congee or oats with milk, 2 eggs, fruit |
| Lunch, ~6-7 h before (main fuelling meal) | 2 bowls rice (~100 g carbs), steamed or braised chicken / fish / tofu (~30-45 g protein), cooked vegetables, little oil and chilli |
| ~3:30 pm | Mantou or fruit bread + 250 mL milk |
| 1.5-2.5 h before (5:30-6:30 pm) | Banana + rice crackers or white bread with honey/jam (~40-80 g carbs); 400-600 mL water; nothing greasy, very spicy, high-fibre or bulky within ~3 h |
| Warm-up and game | Water; 300-500 mL sports drink (~20-30 g carbs) sipped at time-outs and breaks if the pre-game meal was light or he usually fades |
| Half-time | Sips of water or the sports drink above; half a banana or a few lollies only if hungry (our rule) |
| Post-game, by ~10 pm | Recovery meal: rice or noodles + lean protein (~30 g) + vegetables + water (1.25-1.5 L per kg lost, FR-hydration). If team drinks: eat first, alternate with water, keep alcohol light |
| Bedtime | Wind-down routine (FR-sleep); no caffeine after the game; milk/yoghurt only if the post-game meal was small (our rule) |

Caffeine: see FR-supplements (for a 7-9 pm game, skip or use a low dose).

Cue: "Big rice lunch, snack at five, sip, refuel by ten." / 中文："午饭多吃饭，五点加餐，比赛小口喝，十点前吃恢复餐。"

**Check:** Log "legs in 4th quarter" 1-5 and game weigh-in loss; compare games fuelled with the timeline against ones that were not (our rule).

**Source:** Joint Stand 2016 (pre-exercise carbs and fluid; small carb amounts or mouth rinse in 45-75 min high-intensity work, 30-60 g/h in 1-2.5 h stop-start sport; alcohol impairs rehydration and recovery); SDA basketball; Baker et al. 2007, PubMed 17596779 (drink vs water no difference when hydrated); timeline and amounts our rule.

**Evidence level:** Strong (principles); Moderate (applicability of in-game carbs to a 40-min rec game); the timeline is our rule.

### FR-hydration

**Situation:** Big weight drop across a game, dark urine before a game, headaches or cramps after play, salty white crust on clothes, or no sweat test done yet.

**Why:** Basketball skill and sprint drills got worse from about 2 % dehydration (shots fewer, sprint/lateral drills slower, larger as dehydration grew). Sweat rate and sweat sodium vary widely, so a personal test beats a generic number. Over-drinking is also a risk (hyponatraemia).

**What to do (76 kg):**
- Limit: lose < 2 % = **< 1.5 kg** per game; aim < 1 kg (our rule).
- **Sweat test**, twice (one training, one game): empty bladder, weigh in minimal clothing; play; note fluid drunk; towel dry and weigh again. Sweat loss (L) = (pre - post kg) + fluid drunk (L) - urine (L); sweat rate = loss / hours; % change = (pre - post) / pre x 100.
- **After:** replace **1.25-1.5 L per kg lost** (1 kg lost = 1.25-1.5 L over the next hours), with sodium from food or drink; do not restrict salt after big sweat losses. Chinese meals (soy sauce, broth) usually cover the sodium.
- **Sodium during play** only if sweat rate > 1.2 L/h, sweat is salty, or play lasts > 2 h; electrolyte tablets only if his test shows it (our rule).
- Drink to the plan plus thirst; do not force fluid.

**Check:** Game loss < 1 kg on the scale; morning and pre-game urine pale straw.

**Source:** ACSM 2007 (prevent > 2 % loss; individual testing; urine colour); Baker, Dougherty, Chow & Kenney 2007, Med Sci Sports Exerc 39(7):1114-1123, PubMed 17596779 (17 players, decline significant from 2 %; caveat: dehydration induced by 3 h walking in heat); Joint Stand 2016 (1.25-1.5 L/kg, sodium > 1.2 L/h, ~1 g sodium per L sweat on average, over-drinking risk). Typical indoor sweat rates (0.8-1.5 L/h) are unverified; his own test replaces them.

**Evidence level:** Strong (targets and replacement); Moderate (basketball performance effect).

**Stop and get help:** headache, dizziness, nausea or confusion during or after play (possible heat illness or hyponatraemia).

### FR-supplements

**Situation:** He asks about supplements, or the plan considers one. Only AIS Group A items are discussed.

**Why:** Supplements add only a little on top of a good diet, carry a contamination risk, and should be trialled in training first. AIS Group A means **strong evidence for use in specific situations in sport with evidence-based protocols, not "good for everyone"**. Group A performance supplements: caffeine, beta-alanine, dietary nitrate, sodium bicarbonate, creatine, glycerol.

**What to do (76 kg):**
- **Creatine monohydrate: 3 g/day, no loading**, with any meal, plain powder (our rule inside the ISSN 3-5 g/day). It fits repeated sprints, jumps and strength work. Trade-off: it commonly adds ~0.5-2 kg, mostly water in muscle (unverified range); for a player who already feels heavy, skip the loading phase and watch scale and jump tests. Without loading, muscle saturation takes ~3-4 weeks (standard ISSN figure, unverified in full text). Tell the doctor before a blood test (it can raise creatinine); anyone with kidney disease asks a doctor first.
- **Caffeine: 3 mg/kg = ~230 mg about 60 min before**, only when bedtime is more than 6 h away (in practice a game starting before ~6 pm; our rule). For a 7-9 pm game: skip it, or 1-2 mg/kg (75-150 mg, one coffee) and accept a later, lighter sleep; never an energy drink after the game (our rule). Even 6 h is not fully safe: 400 mg taken 6 h before bed cut measured sleep by over 1 h without people noticing (n = 12). Trial in training first.
- **Not worth it now:** beta-alanine (helps 1-4 min efforts, basketball efforts are < 10 s), bicarbonate and glycerol (other sports, gut upset), BCAAs, glutamine, test boosters, fat burners, collagen for performance, most recovery blends, HMB, tart cherry (inconsistent). High-dose vitamin C/E around training may blunt adaptation; food sources are fine. Beetroot juice: optional, mixed evidence; leafy Chinese greens (bok choy, choy sum) are a food source of nitrate.
- Protein powder is a Group A sports food: a convenience tool when a meal is far away, not a requirement. Vitamin D, iron etc. only after a blood test shows a deficiency.
- Buy batch-tested products (HASTA or Informed Sport), check the batch in the Sport Integrity app; an SIA-commissioned 2022 survey found about 1 in 3 of 200 online supplements contained a prohibited substance.

**Check:** Creatine: weekly weight average and jump/sprint tests over 4 weeks; stop if he feels heavier and jumps do not improve (our rule). Caffeine: sleep hours logged on game nights.

**Source:** IOC 2018; AIS Group A page; ISSN Kreider 2017 (creatine efficacy and safety; dosing figures unverified in full text); ISSN Guest 2021 (3-6 mg/kg ~60 min before; team-sport sprints and jumps); Drake et al. 2013, J Clin Sleep Med 9(11):1195-1200, PMC3805807; Trexler 2015, DOI 10.1186/s12970-015-0090-y; SIA https://www.sportintegrity.gov.au/supplements and https://www.sportintegrity.gov.au/news/media-statements/2025-04/sport-supplements-survey

**Evidence level:** Strong (creatine and caffeine for performance); Moderate (caffeine sleep cost, beta-alanine fit, antioxidants); dose choices for him are our rule.

### FR-sleep

**Situation:** Sleep log under 7 h, trouble falling asleep after evening games, irregular wake time, or flat sessions the day after a game.

**Why:** Sleep is the cheapest and biggest recovery lever. Athletes often sleep < 7 h with fragmented sleep, and competition timing disturbs it. In a small, uncontrolled study, basketball players who extended sleep ran faster sprints and shot better.

**What to do:**
- Target **7-9 h**, wake time consistent (+/- 1 h, our rule).
- Before a game: an extra 30-60 min in bed on the 2 nights before (our rule).
- Late-game wind-down (our rule, from the Walsh 2021 toolbox and Drake 2013): no caffeine within 6 h of bed; after the game a cool shower, eat the recovery meal, dim lights, screens down, write tomorrow's to-do list, keep alcohol low; accept falling asleep later but keep the wake time; a 20-30 min early-afternoon nap the next day if possible.
- Bedroom cool, dark, quiet.

Cue: "Same wake time, even after a late game." / 中文："晚场比赛后起床时间也不变。"

**Check:** Log sleep hours nightly; weekly average >= 7 h, game-night loss made up within 2 days (our rule).

**Source:** Hirshkowitz et al. 2015, Sleep Health 1(1):40-43, DOI 10.1016/j.sleh.2014.12.010 (7-9 h); Walsh et al. 2021, Br J Sports Med 55(7):356-368, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2020-102025; Mah et al. 2011, Sleep 34(7):943-950, DOI 10.5665/SLEEP.1132 (n = 11); Drake et al. 2013.

**Evidence level:** Strong (health and the athlete consensus); Weak-Moderate (sleep extension for performance).

### FR-recovery-ranked

**Situation:** He asks what to do for recovery, buys gadgets, takes ice baths after lifting, or stretches to fix soreness.

**Why:** The big levers are sleep, food and sensible load. Passive methods reduce soreness and perceived fatigue a little; massage ranked best for soreness in a large meta-analysis, stretching showed no effect. Cold-water immersion after strength training blunted strength and muscle gains, which is the point of sessions A and D.

**What to do (ranked, our rule built on the evidence):**

| Rank | Method | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sleep 7-9 h | Daily (FR-sleep) |
| 2 | Enough energy, carbs, protein, fluid | Daily (FR-energy-by-day, FR-protein, FR-hydration) |
| 3 | Sensible load progression, planned easy days | FR-load-progression |
| 4 | Light active recovery: walk, easy bike or swim 15-30 min + mobility | Day after the game, not a hard run |
| 5 | Massage, incl. self-massage tools | Nice to have; professional once a fortnight if budget allows |
| 6 | Compression (calf sleeves, tights) | Optional, a few hours after a game |
| 7 | Cold-water immersion | After games only, 10-15 min at ~10-15 °C if it helps; **not within ~4-6 h after A or D** during the block |
| 8 | Foam rolling | 30-60 s per muscle in the warm-up, not as a cure |
| 9 | Sauna | If enjoyed; not counted as recovery; rehydrate |
| 10 | Static stretching | For range of motion, not for soreness |

A cold shower for comfort is fine any time.

**Check:** He spends his recovery effort in rank order: sleep and food logged before any gadget (our rule).

**Source:** Dupuy et al. 2018, Front Physiol 9:403, DOI 10.3389/fphys.2018.00403 (99 studies; massage best for DOMS and fatigue; stretching no effect); Roberts et al. 2015, J Physiol 593(18):4285-4301, DOI 10.1113/JP270570; Grgic 2022, PubMed 35068365 (ES -0.23 on strength); Piñero et al. 2024, Eur J Sport Sci 24(2):177-189; Hill et al. 2014, Br J Sports Med 48(18):1340-1346, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2013-092456 (compression); Wiewelhove et al. 2019, Front Physiol 10:376, DOI 10.3389/fphys.2019.00376 (foam rolling); Herbert et al. 2011 Cochrane, DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD004577.pub3 (stretching; cited from memory in the notes, unverified); sauna review https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41032138/ (unclear).

**Evidence level:** Moderate (most methods; many small studies); Weak/unclear (sauna); the ranking itself is our rule.

### FR-soreness-vs-pain

**Situation:** Calf soreness after running or sprints (worst on the treadmill), knee aches after games, or any pain note in a session log.

**Why:** Delayed-onset muscle soreness (DOMS) after new or eccentric work is normal; pain that is sharp, local, one-sided or rising needs a professional. In Achilles tendinopathy patients, continued running/jumping with pain kept at or below ~5/10 during activity and settled by the next morning gave results similar to rest. This KB does not diagnose.

**What to do:**

| Likely DOMS (normal) | Possible injury: get it checked if it persists |
|---|---|
| Starts 12-24 h after, peaks 24-72 h, gone by ~5 days | Sharp pain during activity, or a pop/snap |
| Diffuse ache over the muscle belly, both legs similar | One clear spot, one side, tender on a tendon or bone |
| Eases as he warms up | Gets worse as he keeps going |
| No or mild swelling | Swelling, bruising, warmth, limping |
| Follows new or harder work | Returns after every session or does not settle in a week |

- **Pain score rule (our rule):** score calf and knee pain 0-10 the night after and the next morning. Next morning <= 3/10 and back to baseline in 24-48 h: keep training and building strength. Rising week to week: cut jumping/running volume and see a physio. During activity, stay at or below ~5/10 (Silbernagel model, with a physio).
- **Red flags -> sports physiotherapist or doctor:** sharp or clearly localised pain; swelling; pain at night or at rest that does not ease; limping or cannot bear weight; pain rising during a session; knee locks, catches or gives way; cannot fully straighten the knee; hot, red joint or fever; numbness or tingling; pain worsening over 2-3 weeks despite less load.
- **Same-day doctor / emergency:** a pop or "kicked in the back of the leg" feeling with inability to push up on the toes; a calf that is swollen, warm, red or tender at rest, especially after a flight or long sitting, or with breathlessness or chest pain (possible blood clot).
- Capacity work (heel raises, isometrics) belongs to `movement_kb.md` / the program; this entry only gates it.

**Check:** Morning pain scores in the log; trend flat or falling across weeks.

**Source:** DOMS table and red flags: general clinical teaching as written in the research notes (our rule); Silbernagel et al. 2007, Am J Sports Med 35(6):897-906, DOI 10.1177/0363546506298279; Collins et al. 2018, Br J Sports Med 52(18):1170-1178, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2018-099397 (exercise therapy for patellofemoral pain, context).

**Evidence level:** Moderate (pain-monitoring model); the 3/10 next-morning threshold is our rule.

### FR-load-progression

**Situation:** New sprint, plyo or running volume is being added; soreness or pain scores climb; 2-3 warning signs persist; week 4 or week 8 of the block.

**Why:** Big sudden spikes in one type of load (sprint metres, jump contacts, running minutes) on tissue that is not yet adapted are the practical risk. The 10 % rule did not prevent injuries in a novice-runner RCT, and the acute:chronic workload ratio is not a reliable injury predictor, so the rule here is simple tracking and small steps.

**What to do (our rule unless cited):**
- Log each session in one line: date, type, minutes, session RPE (0-10), sprint metres or jump contacts, next-morning calf and knee pain (0-10), sleep hours.
- Increase **one load type at a time, ~10-20 % per week**; hold or drop it when next-morning pain goes above 3/10.
- Plyos start low: ~40-60 foot contacts per session; no full plyo program on top of the game week in week 1.
- Keep >= 48 h between the two highest-impact sessions (game and D) where possible; no hardest jump/sprint session the day after a game in weeks 1-4.
- **Deload:** weeks 4 and 8, cut volume ~40-50 % (fewer sets, jumps, sprints), keep intensity moderate-high, keep the game. Deload early if 2-3 of these last more than a few days: next-morning pain > 3/10, resting heart rate clearly up, worse sleep, low motivation, jump or sprint tests clearly worse.

**Check:** Weekly log shows only one load type rising; pain scores stay <= 3/10; jump/sprint tests hold or improve after each deload.

**Source:** Buist et al. 2008, Am J Sports Med 36(1):33-39, DOI 10.1177/0363546507307505 (20.8 % vs 20.3 % injuries); ACWR critique: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8138569/ and Impellizzeri et al. 2020, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7534938/ ; deload: common strength and conditioning practice.

**Evidence level:** Moderate (what does not work); Weak-Moderate (deload practice); thresholds are our rule.

### FR-body-comp

**Situation:** He says he feels heavy or wants to lose fat; a plan is about to include a deficit; weight is falling during the block.

**Why:** Sprint acceleration and jumping depend on force relative to body mass, so losing fat helps only if strength holds; losing muscle lowers power. A deficit while tissues adapt to new sprint/plyo load costs recovery and adaptation. "Heavy" legs and late fade can also come from low fuel, poor sleep, low fitness or sore legs; fix those first. BMI 25.4 says nothing about fat in someone who lifts.

**What to do:**
- **During the 8-week block: no cut. Eat at maintenance** (option A); a very slow drift (about -0.25 kg/week, small deficit on rest days only) is the most the block allows, and only if he chooses it (our rule).
- **After the block, if he wants it:** slow loss, <= 0.5 kg/week (~0.7 % body mass), ~300-500 kcal/day below maintenance (our estimate), protein 1.8-2.2 g/kg (~135-165 g), carbs kept around sessions, deficit taken from oil, fried food, alcohol and sweet drinks on rest days.
- Judge by weekly average weight, waist (< 94 cm is a widely used health threshold, unverified), photos, and jump/sprint tests, not BMI.

**Check:** Weekly weight average within ~0.5 kg of the start during the block (our rule); jump/sprint tests not worse.

**Source:** Joint Stand 2016 (plan weight loss away from key competition; < 1 %/week preserves fat-free mass and performance; higher protein in a deficit); Garthe et al. 2011, Int J Sport Nutr Exerc Metab 21(2):97-104, DOI 10.1123/ijsnem.21.2.97 (0.7 %/week gained lean mass and strength, 1.4 %/week did not; small study); Morton et al. 2018.

**Evidence level:** Strong (slow-loss principle); Moderate (Garthe rates); the no-cut-in-block choice is our rule.

## Plate method (our rule)

Adapted in the notes from the US Olympic & Paralympic "athlete plates" idea and the Joint Stand periodisation. Think of a dinner plate, or the rice bowl plus shared dishes.

| Day type | Carbs (rice, noodles, buns, potato) | Protein (meat, fish, tofu, egg) | Vegetables | Oil |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rest day | 1/4 plate (~1 bowl rice) | 1/4 plate (1-1.5 palms, ~30 g protein) | 1/2 plate | small |
| Training day (A-D) | 1/3 plate (1-1.5 bowls) | 1/3 plate | 1/3 plate | moderate |
| Game day / day before | 1/2 plate (1.5-2 bowls) | 1/4 plate | 1/4 plate, cooked (no big raw salads before the game) | low before the game |

Hand portions per meal: protein 1-1.5 palms, carbs 1-2 cupped fists, vegetables 2 fists, oil 1 thumb. At shared meals, serve your protein first, then rice, then vegetables. Ask for "少油" (less oil) and sauce on the side. Steamed, poached, braised, clear soups, boiled dumplings and steamed fish more often; deep-fried, sweet-and-sour, salt-and-pepper fried, oily fried rice/chow mein and hot-pot oil bases less often.

Food values used below (approximate, AFCD/labels): 1 bowl cooked rice (~190 g) ~52 g carbs, ~4-5 g protein; 1 cup cooked noodles (~200 g) ~45-55 g carbs; 1 mantou ~40-45 g carbs; 1 boiled dumpling ~5-6 g carbs, ~2-3 g protein; 1 egg ~6-7 g protein; firm tofu 100 g ~12-15 g protein; cooked chicken breast 100 g ~30 g, lean beef/pork 100 g ~26-30 g, white fish 100 g ~20-23 g protein; milk 250 mL ~8-9 g protein, ~12 g carbs; unsweetened soy milk 250 mL ~7-9 g protein (sweetened adds ~10-15 g sugar); banana ~25 g carbs. Baozi are not in the notes: count one like a mantou plus a little protein (rough).

## One-week recovery template (our rule)

Assumes a **Friday evening game**; session letters follow the 8-week program. If the game is **Saturday**, keep Sunday as active recovery and move D to Monday, A to Tuesday, B to Wednesday (no hard jump/sprint session the day after a game).

| Day | Session | Fuel (FR-energy-by-day) | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mon | **A** braking strength / plyo | 4-5 g/kg carbs; ~30 g protein after | No ice bath within 4-6 h; pre-bed milk; morning pain check Tue |
| Tue | **B** court movement: speed, deceleration (~40-60 foot contacts early in the block) | 4-5 g/kg; 4-5 pm snack before | Easy evening walk optional |
| Wed | Rest, or 20-30 min easy bike/walk + mobility | 3-3.5 g/kg; protein steady | Extra sleep; massage if any |
| Thu | **C** skill with trainer: short, crisp, low volume | ~5 g/kg (top up) | Early night; sleep banking night 1 (+30-60 min) |
| Fri | **Game** (evening) | 5-6 g/kg, FR-game-day timeline; sweat test | Post-game meal + fluids; cold water immersion optional; wind-down; same wake time Sat |
| Sat | Active recovery: walk, swim or easy bike 15-30 min + mobility | Normal; rehydrate | Compression optional; nap if possible; pain score morning |
| Sun | **D** power + conditioning (jumps, sprints, med ball, short intervals) | 4-5 g/kg | No ice bath within 4-6 h; pain score Mon morning |

Weeks 4 and 8: same days, volume cut ~40-50 % (FR-load-progression). Daily constants: creatine 3 g if he uses it, 4 x ~30 g protein, 7-9 h sleep, weekly weight average.
